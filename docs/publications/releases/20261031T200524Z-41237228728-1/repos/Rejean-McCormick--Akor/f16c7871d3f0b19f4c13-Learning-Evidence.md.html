# Learning Evidence vs Mission Progress

Akor bridges two legitimate but **different** kinds of evidence.

## Operational evidence (Orgo)

Examples: a role received its authorized instruction, a task was submitted, a required hand-off occurred, or a mission was closed. These observations help the mission proceed, but **do not certify curricular mastery**.

## Pedagogical evidence (Ekole)

Examples: a learner arranged twelve objects into equal groups and explained the multiplication, compared two strategies, corrected an error, or justified a joint decision. Pedagogical review requires the objective, conditions, degree of independence and any supports used.

| Question | Operational (Orgo) | Educational (Ekole) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Was a step completed? | Yes: authoritative workflow state | May reference completion |
| Did the learner understand the concept? | Not necessarily | Teacher assessment supported by work |
| Who can see detailed student reasoning? | Minimal refs as needed | Authorized teacher/learner views |
| Can success equal mastery? | No | Only through appropriate evaluation |

## Proposed evidence handoff

1. Orgo accepts/records the permitted operational contribution.
2. Akor conveys the smallest necessary event/reference under consent and authorization.
3. Ekole stores or references learner work, objective IDs, source versions, support context and review status.
4. A teacher verifies and interprets the learning evidence.
5. Orgo receives a minimal acknowledgement **only if a further operational step depends on that review**.

Do not place answers, diagnostic data or student dossiers inside arbitrary Orgo Task fields. Do not create a group mastery score based solely on whether a group reached the final screen.
