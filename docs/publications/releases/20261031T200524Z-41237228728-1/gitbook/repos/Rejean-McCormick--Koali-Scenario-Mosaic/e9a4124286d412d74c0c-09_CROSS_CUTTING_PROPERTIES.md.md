# Cross-cutting properties

These properties are not separate product categories. They change the value or operating model of many scenarios.

## Multilingual
Most valuable when one knowledge object must remain coherent across communities that do not share a language: humanitarian work, public services, research networks, education, agriculture, diplomacy, migration, culture, and international organizations.

## Offline / intermittent connectivity
Important where the network is absent, expensive, unreliable, or likely to fail exactly when coordination matters: field research, ships, mines, remote schools, disaster response, rural infrastructure, humanitarian operations, and critical sites.

## Local-first / sensitive data
Important when an organization needs to control where knowledge and operational data reside: research and development, government, health-adjacent settings, legal work, industrial operations, HR, finance, and infrastructure.

## AI-optional
Core knowledge, search, graph, workflow, collaboration, archive, permissions, synchronization, and deterministic rendering should not be explained as requiring an external LLM. AI can augment the environment without becoming the authority or single point of failure.

## Public-facing
Applies when knowledge, decisions, or outcomes must be explained outside the working group. UCKK and media workflows become more relevant here.

## High-consequence
In safety-, medical-, legal-, financial-, aviation-, industrial-, or emergency-critical settings, Koali should be framed as supporting context, coordination, evidence, and memory—not replacing certified systems, licensed judgment, or statutory authority.
