# Sociotechnical Operating System

> **Reading model:** The main path explains what the kOA ecosystem enables. The technical reference explains how kOA-Linux hosts, isolates, verifies, and operates those capabilities. Product-specific details remain in each subsystem's own documentation.

## Governance as runtime

A conventional platform hosts content or interaction. A **Sociotechnical Operating System** runs the combined social and technical rules that make collective action possible:

- roles and permissions;
- integrity and validation rules;
- decision protocols;
- accountability workflows;
- audit and recourse;
- semantic and language infrastructure;
- operational routing, escalation, and closure;
- local and offline continuity.

The social structure is not an external policy document added after the software is built. Roles, norms, authority, consent, and review are part of the operating model.

## Why a system of systems

No single platform should own knowledge, public deliberation, organizational execution, language, culture, identity, media, and host privilege at once. kOA separates these functions into systems with explicit boundaries.

This supports **integration without contamination**: systems exchange artifacts, requests, events, and receipts without silently absorbing each other's authority or databases.

## Conditions of legitimacy

Operational power is acceptable only when it remains:

- inspectable and auditable;
- contestable and revisable;
- explicit about the difference between credibility and decision result;
- portable and locally operable;
- resistant to platform capture;
- multilingual without flattening differences;
- bounded by consent, rights, and cultural authority;
- capable of safe failure rather than hidden substitution.

## Role of kOA-Linux

kOA-Linux is the technical runtime beneath this social architecture. It supplies the host-level conditions—profiles, isolation, trust, resources, activation, offline behavior, backup, and recovery—without claiming ownership of the product logic of Konnaxion, Orgo, Ariane, SemantiK Architect, SenTient, or other independent systems.
