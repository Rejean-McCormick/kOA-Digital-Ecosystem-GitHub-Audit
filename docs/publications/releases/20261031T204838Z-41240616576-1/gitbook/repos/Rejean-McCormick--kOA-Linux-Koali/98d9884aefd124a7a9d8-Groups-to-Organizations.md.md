# From Groups to Organizations

> **Authority and status:** This wiki is an explanatory navigation layer. The repository contracts, accepted ADRs, normative documents, and executable validators remain authoritative for kOA-Linux Operating System. Public initkoa.org pages explain the wider kOA ecosystem and the product capabilities provided by its independently documented subsystems.

## A cross-system outcome

“Turn a group of people into an organization” is not one button or one database table. It is a progression from shared attention to legitimate structure and accountable execution. In kOA, this outcome spans Konnaxion, governance rules and Orgo.

## The progression

### 1. Gather around a subject, place or need

Konnaxion provides spaces for learning, collaboration, projects, public discussion and shared resources.

### 2. Form a shared purpose

The group develops a mission, problem statement, proposal or project. Relevant knowledge can be packaged and cited through Kristal.

### 3. Make membership and participation visible

The group identifies participants, contribution rules, consent, rights and the scope of public or private information.

### 4. Establish legitimate rules

The group adopts a charter, decision procedure, delegation model and review process. Advisory expertise can inform decisions without silently replacing the democratic baseline.

### 5. Create operational structure

Orgo represents the organization, roles, permissions, teams, role inboxes, cases, tasks, response windows and escalation ladders.

### 6. Execute and learn

Accepted decisions become owned work. Outcomes, unresolved issues, audits and recurring patterns return to the collective for review.

## What the operating system contributes

kOA-Linux provides the separated public and private environments, identity and trust services, policy evaluation, local databases, messaging boundaries, artifact storage, offline operation, backup and recovery.

## What remains a product contract

The exact onboarding flow, legal status, membership lifecycle, voting rules, role taxonomy and dissolution procedure must be defined by Konnaxion, Orgo and governance specifications. The operating system must not invent those rules.
