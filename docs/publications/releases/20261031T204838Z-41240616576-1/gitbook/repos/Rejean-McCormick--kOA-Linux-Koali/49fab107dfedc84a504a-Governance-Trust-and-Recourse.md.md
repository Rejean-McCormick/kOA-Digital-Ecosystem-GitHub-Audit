# Governance, Trust, Rights, and Recourse

> **Authority and status:** This wiki is an explanatory navigation layer. The repository contracts, accepted ADRs, normative documents, and executable validators remain authoritative for kOA-Linux Operating System. Public initkoa.org pages explain the wider kOA ecosystem and the product capabilities provided by its independently documented subsystems.

## Governance as an operating constraint

Governance is not a decorative policy page. It defines which actions are legitimate, which data may be disclosed, who may authorize sensitive transitions, how decisions are recorded, and how people can contest or exit the system.

## Core safeguards

- **Non-domination:** operators and technical administrators do not receive unlimited civic or organizational authority.
- **Auditability:** important outcomes can be traced to inputs, rules and authorized roles.
- **Contestability:** decisions and automated outcomes expose a review path.
- **Fail-closed integrity:** failed verification blocks authority rather than silently weakening rules.
- **Selective disclosure:** institutions can be accountable without publishing every personal or operational detail.
- **Consent and cultural rights:** access, use, withdrawal and AI restrictions follow explicit policy.
- **Right to exit:** people and organizations can export data, rules and portable artifacts.

## Trust layers

The system separates:

- identity of people and services;
- role or mandate authority;
- artifact integrity and signer trust;
- recognition of knowledge assertions;
- permission to disclose data;
- authorization to perform an operation;
- host privilege.

One form of trust does not automatically grant the others.

## Receipts and recourse

Critical decisions and operations should produce machine-readable receipts that identify the policy, inputs, decision, actor, time and relevant evidence. Recourse may reopen a case, suspend an action, create a review task or activate an appeal procedure.
