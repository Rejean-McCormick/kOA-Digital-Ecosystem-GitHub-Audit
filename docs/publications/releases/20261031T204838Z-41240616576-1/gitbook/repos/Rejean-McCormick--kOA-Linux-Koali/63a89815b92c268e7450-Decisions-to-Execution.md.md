# From Decisions to Execution

> **Authority and status:** This wiki is an explanatory navigation layer. The repository contracts, accepted ADRs, normative documents, and executable validators remain authoritative for kOA-Linux Operating System. Public initkoa.org pages explain the wider kOA ecosystem and the product capabilities provided by its independently documented subsystems.

## Why decisions often fail

Many institutions can discuss and vote but cannot reliably convert an outcome into accountable follow-through. Responsibility remains implicit, deadlines disappear, and closure is replaced by status meetings or message threads.

Orgo supplies the execution layer, while kOA-Linux supplies the environment and governance boundary.

## Execution contract

An operationalized decision should identify:

- the decision or mandate reference;
- the responsible function or role;
- affected organizations, people or locations;
- required cases and tasks;
- inputs and expected outputs;
- response and completion windows;
- escalation rules;
- confidentiality and visibility;
- approval or human-review gates;
- closure criteria and evidence;
- review, appeal or revocation conditions.

## Signal-to-closure lifecycle

1. A decision, report, request or incident becomes a signal.
2. The signal is normalized and classified.
3. A case provides durable context and ownership.
4. Tasks express executable work.
5. Work routes to functions or roles rather than relying only on named individuals.
6. Time windows make delay visible.
7. Escalation occurs under explicit rules.
8. Closure records what happened and what remains open.
9. Patterns create review work.

## Automation

Tasks may be performed by humans, deterministic tools, constrained agents or hybrid methods. Automation must remain allowlisted, resource-bounded, attributable and subject to the same approval and evidence requirements.
