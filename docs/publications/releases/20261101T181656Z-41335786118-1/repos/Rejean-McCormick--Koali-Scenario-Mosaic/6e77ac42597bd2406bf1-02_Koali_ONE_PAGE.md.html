# Koali in one page

**Koali, the Sociotechnical Operating System**

## One sentence

> **Koali is a knowledge-to-action environment that connects what people know, who can contribute, what gets decided, what must be done, and what is learned afterward.**

## The problem it addresses

In most organizations, continuity is fragmented:

- knowledge is in documents, databases, inboxes, and people’s heads;
- collaboration happens in messaging and meetings;
- decisions are recorded inconsistently;
- work moves to task or operational systems;
- learning lives in LMSs or informal mentoring;
- media and public communication become detached from their sources;
- institutional memory is incomplete.

Koali is useful where those pieces need to remain connected.

## What people do with it

**Find** information, prior work, experts, resources, and relevant context.

**Understand** a situation by connecting sources, evidence, history, relationships, and uncertainty.

**Learn & share** methods, knowledge, skills, lessons, and situated know-how.

**Collaborate & create** research, designs, policies, projects, teams, and shared understanding.

**Choose & govern** by comparing options, making disagreements visible, documenting reasons, and preserving legitimacy.

**Organize & act** by turning decisions or needs into responsibilities, tasks, dependencies, verification, and closure.

**Respond & coordinate** when incidents, crises, disruptions, or field conditions change faster than normal planning cycles.

**Remember & disseminate** by turning outcomes into reusable memory and, when appropriate, public learning or multimedia.

## The main system roles

**Kristal — what we know.** Semantic knowledge, provenance, relationships, versions, evidence, memory.

**Konnaxion — what people do together with that knowledge.** Discovery, learning, contribution, collaboration, discussion, team formation, deliberation.

**Orgo — what must happen next.** Cases, responsibilities, tasks, routing, dependencies, evidence, escalation, closure.

**UCKK — what we teach or disseminate more broadly.** Courses, media, public learning, reusable knowledge channels.

## Important properties

Koali can be presented as **multilingual, local/offline-capable, AI-optional, and compatible with sensitive-data settings** where the underlying documented architecture supports those claims. These are cross-cutting properties, not separate use cases.

## The simplest mental model

> **The professions change. The organizations change. The problems change. The transitions keep recurring. Koali is designed around those transitions.**
