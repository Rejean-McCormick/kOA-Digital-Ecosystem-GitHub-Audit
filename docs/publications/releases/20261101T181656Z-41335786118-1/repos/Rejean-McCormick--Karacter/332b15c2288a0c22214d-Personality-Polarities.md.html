# Personality Polarities

Karacter's defining principle: **a tendency is not automatically a strength or a flaw**. Its usefulness depends on circumstances.

## Two linked 0–6 scales

Each axis has two named poles. The two sliders are linked:

```text
Prudence  [5 / 6]    Audace  [1 / 6]    5 + 1 = 6
Prudence  [3 / 6]    Audace  [3 / 6]    3 + 3 = 6
Prudence  [0 / 6]    Audace  [6 / 6]    0 + 6 = 6
```

Moving either slider automatically updates the other. No `+` or `−` ranking is used, and **neither end is morally superior**.

## What a score means—and does not mean

A score indicates a **habitual preference within the named context**, not ability, intelligence, courage, mental health, or human worth. `Prudence 5 / Audace 1` means this character usually *prefers a cautious approach* in the situations covered by that axis. It does **not** mean the character cannot be courageous.

This is an intentionally simplified creative model. Real people can be highly cautious in planning **and** audacious in action, and many named qualities are not true psychological opposites. Choose meaningful contexts rather than treating the axis as scientific measurement.

## Every pole has trade-offs

An axis can store:

- A **left and right label**.
- A context in which the axis applies.
- Visual cues for each pole.
- Situations where **each pole** may help.
- Situations where **each pole** may complicate things.

Example:

| Pole | Can help when… | Can complicate when… |
|---|---|---|
| Prudence | A risky decision needs verification | Urgent action is required |
| Audace | The team must explore an unfamiliar option | The consequences are poorly understood |

These are narrative possibilities, not verdicts.

## Editing axes

Open **Understand**, switch to **Studio** for the full profile, then customize any axis. You can rename both poles, revise contextual/visual descriptions, create new axes, remove axes and move axes **up or down**. **Explorer** shows a simplified subset of up to four axes; hidden axes are not deleted.

John Doe begins with **zero axes** by design. Add only what serves the character.

## How polarities affect simulations and art

The simulation engine uses these preferences as **one input** among values, fears, relationships, scenario stakes and story memories. It proposes multiple paths, not a single inevitable response. The image prompt uses selected visual cues to support a recognizable design; a still portrait cannot prove a psychological trait.
