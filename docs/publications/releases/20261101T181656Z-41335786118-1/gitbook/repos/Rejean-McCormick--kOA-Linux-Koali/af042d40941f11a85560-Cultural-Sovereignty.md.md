# Cultural Sovereignty

> **Reading model:** The main path explains what the kOA ecosystem enables. The technical reference explains how kOA-Linux hosts, isolates, verifies, and operates those capabilities. Product-specific details remain in each subsystem's own documentation.

## Culture as governed memory

Cultural sovereignty requires more than a repository of files. A cultural system must preserve the relationships that make a work meaningful:

- creator and community identity;
- provenance and lineage;
- cultural context;
- rights, restrictions, licences, and attribution;
- consent and withdrawal;
- authority to interpret, display, reuse, translate, or train AI;
- moderation, disputes, revisions, and decision records.

## Kreative and Konservation

Within Konnaxion:

- **Kreative** is the cultural memory, collaboration, discovery, and creator-network layer.
- **Konservation** is the preservation and archive-oriented submodule.
- **Kontact** connects creators, communities, institutions, collaborators, patrons, and opportunities.

Detailed product documentation belongs in the [Konnaxion Wiki](https://github.com/Rejean-McCormick/Konnaxion/wiki), especially [Konservation](https://github.com/Rejean-McCormick/Konnaxion/wiki/Konservation) and [Kontact](https://github.com/Rejean-McCormick/Konnaxion/wiki/Kontact).

## Integration without appropriation

External collections can be mapped into shared structures while preserving source identity, stewardship, rights, and access restrictions. Interoperability must not erase the authority of museums, archives, Indigenous governance bodies, artists, or communities.

AI may suggest tags, descriptions, translations, accessibility text, or duplicate candidates. It must not silently define cultural meaning or override community authority.
