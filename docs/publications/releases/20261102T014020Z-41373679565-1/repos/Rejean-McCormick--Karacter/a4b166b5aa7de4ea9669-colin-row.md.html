# Colin Row

> **Kind:** continuity-mode · **Source status:** CANON / CANON_SOURCE_CORPUS

A continuity mode of King Klown: incarceration after a pharmacy break-in while trying to save his son, collective notebooks and co-design. The superseded police-strike account is not used.

## Creative proposal — not established canon

The loss in the source belongs to its fictional narrative layer; no civil or real-world identity is inferred.

### Character identity

**Role:** Prison-era co-designer who turns exclusion into collective knowledge
**Keywords:** observant, solidarity, co-design, listening, prison-phase

### Inner world

- **Motivation:** Protect the voices of those excluded from institutions and turn lived problems into shared designs.
- **Values:** equal voice, dignity, solidarity, accuracy, non-appropriation
- **Fear:** That other people’s accounts will again be spoken over or appropriated.
- **Typical reaction:** Slows the conversation, asks who decides, what is missing and what comes next; records rather than takes credit.

### Narrative design

- **Desire:** Carry the shared notebook and its collective inventions beyond the prison walls.
- **Need:** Accept that the project belongs to its contributors, not to one founder.
- **Limiting belief:** Unless someone takes responsibility for every thread, the group’s work will disappear.
- **Contradiction:** Wants to organize others without writing their words for them.
- **Turning point:** The notebook leaves the prison while he remains inside.
- **Possible growth:** Become a steward of distributed authorship and trust the work to travel without him.

### Visual direction — prompt suggestion, no source portrait

Fictional prison-era portrait: practical worn jacket, a well-used notebook and graphite-stained fingertips; composed gaze and unadorned materials. Avoid sensational prison imagery.

**Anchors:** shared notebook, modest layered clothing, focused attentive posture

**Exclusions:** handcuffs as identity, sensational violence
