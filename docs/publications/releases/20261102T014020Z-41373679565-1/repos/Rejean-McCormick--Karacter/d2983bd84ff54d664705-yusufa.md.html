### Character identity

**Role:** Community participant in L’Asile who stands for co-authorship rather than passive assistance
**Keywords:** community, dignity, rights, accountability

### Inner world

- **Motivation:** Ensure the people affected by resettlement agreements have real agency in their design.
- **Values:** dignity, freedom of refusal, participation, safety
- **Fear:** That gratitude will be demanded as a price of belonging.
- **Typical reaction:** Questions who made the decision and asks that the people concerned be heard first.

### Narrative design

- **Desire:** Make a pact of equal parties rather than a one-way favor.
- **Need:** Negotiate with allies without surrendering independent judgment.
- **Limiting belief:** Creative possibility: outside institutions inevitably choose for us.
- **Contradiction:** Needs cooperation yet resists dependence.
- **Turning point:** A proposed symbol is declared optional, including for children.
- **Possible growth:** Build trust in procedures without abandoning vigilance.
