# Vision and Name

## Why “Akor”?

**Akor** is a deliberate spelling inspired by the French word *accord*—agreement, harmony, tuning, and acting in concert.

The name carries **two equally important meanings**:

1. **Human accord.** Children participate in a shared mission; they meet, listen, explain, coordinate, disagree respectfully, and find a way forward together.
2. **Technical accord.** Distinct systems exchange only the data and authority they are entitled to exchange. Their contracts fit together without erasing their identities.

The human meaning is the reason the technical bridge exists. Akor should never be described merely as an API router.

## Product statement

**Akor enables children to learn in concert through role-based, interdependent missions that are safe, inclusive, teacher-governed, and linked to learning goals.**

## The distinction that matters

A group is not automatically collaborative. Four children completing four identical worksheets in one room are *co-located*, but their work does not depend on one another. An Akor mission should provide **positive interdependence** while protecting individual agency: a learner's contribution genuinely helps the group, and no child can be permanently trapped waiting for someone else.

Akor's promise is not that every activity must be collaborative. Teachers should be able to choose solo, pair, small-group, or facilitated whole-class formats according to the learning objective and classroom reality.

## Design values

- **Belonging without conformity.** Children may have different roles, abilities, languages, interests, or ways of participating.
- **Voice and agency.** Every participant has decisions to make, not just tasks to obey.
- **Cooperation without forced disclosure.** Private supports and personal circumstances are not announced to teammates.
- **Communication as learning.** The exchange of information, reasoning, and constructive feedback are part of the educational experience.
- **No manufactured exclusion.** Withholding necessary information can be a narrative device only when access is fair, recoverable, authorized, and age-appropriate.
- **Teacher judgment remains central.** A completed operational task does not award grades or certify mastery.
- **Fiction clearly remains fiction.** Simulated money, business, materials, and characters are not permission for real-world transactions or contact.

## A short explanation for different audiences

**For teachers:** Akor helps turn a lesson into a cooperative mission where each child has a meaningful role, and where the teacher can monitor participation and learning without reducing them to one group score.

**For learners:** *You have a role. Your ideas can help the team. Talk, try, help, and discover together.*

**For developers:** Akor is a bounded integration and interaction layer. It bridges versioned mission content and Orgo-authorized state to Ekole presentations and evidence, without becoming an identity provider, workflow engine, or curriculum authority.
