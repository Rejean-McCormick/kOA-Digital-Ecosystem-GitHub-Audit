# Inner World and Relationships

A convincing character is more than their slider positions. Their desires, fears, contradictions and relationships explain **why** they may choose one action over another.

## Inner world and narrative core

Use **Understand** to describe:

| Field | Creative question |
|---|---|
| **Motivation** | What usually gives this character momentum? |
| **Values** | What matters even when there is a cost? |
| **Fear** | What loss or uncertainty is especially difficult? |
| **Habitual reaction** | What is the first pattern we tend to see? |
| **Desire** | What do they consciously want? |
| **Need** | What might they need to learn or accept? |
| **Limiting belief** | What assumption is holding them back? |
| **Contradiction** | Where do their intentions and actions conflict? |
| **Turning point** | What event might challenge their worldview? |
| **Possible growth** | What change could follow? |

These fields are **author-created interpretations**; Karacter does not infer an objective diagnosis.

## Example: a paradox for King Klown

- **Desire:** protect the collective's freedom.
- **Method:** influence outcomes through playful misdirection.
- **Contradiction:** defending others' freedom while quietly influencing their choices.

This tension is narratively interesting even if his linked polarities are balanced. It is an **illustrative creative reading**, not a declaration that every source text confirms the same interpretation.

## Relationships

In **Understand → Studio**, add a link from one character to another. Supported relationship types include **friend, ally, mentor, protégé, family, rival, adversary, complex,** and **custom**. You can describe context, history, trust and sources of tension.

Relations link character **IDs**, not display names. Two characters can have the same name but remain different records. Deleting a character also affects connected relationships; back up before destructive edits.

## Relationships and simulations

In **Simulate**, optionally choose another character involved in the situation. Relationship context and approved events may influence candidate reactions. The engine does **not** automatically rewrite the relationship when it proposes a response; the author decides which event becomes canon.

## Growth and milestones

A milestone can describe an event and a possible shift in behavior or belief. Applying growth is a separate explicit action that updates the character's **limiting belief**, with an undo option. Polarity scores do not move automatically because a story event happened.
