# Collective Intelligence to Governance

> **Authority and status:** This wiki is an explanatory navigation layer. The repository contracts, accepted ADRs, normative documents, and executable validators remain authoritative for kOA-Linux Operating System. Public initkoa.org pages explain the wider kOA ecosystem and the product capabilities provided by its independently documented subsystems.

## The objective

Collective intelligence is useful only when it improves understanding without hiding legitimacy. Governance becomes operational only when accepted outcomes can be converted into bounded authority, responsibilities, tasks, deadlines, review and recourse.

## Konnaxion's role

Konnaxion supports learning, structured deliberation, proposals, argument, drafting and collective decision interfaces. Kollective Intelligence can expose multiple transparent readings of a decision while keeping the democratic baseline visible.

Smart Vote and EkoH-style readings may show domain-bounded competence signals. They are advisory views unless an explicit constitutional rule grants a specific effect. They do not silently reallocate decision rights.

## Governance layer

The governance layer evaluates:

- which procedure applies;
- whether quorum or thresholds are satisfied;
- which authority recognizes the result;
- what scope and duration the decision has;
- which rights, consent or disclosure limits apply;
- how the decision can be contested, reviewed or revoked;
- whether the result may produce a mandate or operational authorization.

## Orgo's role

Orgo turns an accepted outcome into accountable work:

```text
decision or mandate
    ↓
responsible function or role
    ↓
case and tasks
    ↓
response windows and escalation
    ↓
explicit closure and evidence
    ↓
review and institutional learning
```

## Critical separation

A vote or collective reading does not directly become root access, a signing key, a database write or an unbounded automated action. Policy evaluation, authorization and technical privilege remain separate boundaries.
