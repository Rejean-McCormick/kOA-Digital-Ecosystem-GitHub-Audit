# Collaborative Learning Principles

Akor is built around the idea that **children learn through meaningful interaction**, not through the mere presence of other users.

## What makes a mission collaborative?

1. **Shared purpose.** There is a common problem or outcome worth pursuing.
2. **Distinct, meaningful contributions.** Each role brings information, choices, work, or perspective that matters.
3. **A reason to exchange.** Children need to ask, explain, compare, propose, challenge, or combine ideas.
4. **Joint commitment.** The result depends on a real group decision, not only an aggregation of isolated scores.
5. **Individual dignity.** Everyone has a voice, and one child's difference or delay does not become a public penalty.
6. **Visible learning.** Teachers can distinguish who reasoned, what support was used, and what each learner actually demonstrated.

## Interaction patterns worth designing

| Pattern | What children do | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ask and clarify | Request missing information; check understanding | “How many trays does the baker need?” |
| Explain and teach | Show reasoning using words or objects | Arrange counters in equal groups |
| Compare perspectives | Detect and resolve different estimates | Farmer counts loaves; accountant checks inventory |
| Negotiate | Select between defensible options | Change order size or number of batches |
| Hand off | Supply a result another role needs | Baker submits the tray plan |
| Co-create | Combine contributions into one artifact | Produce an order sheet together |
| Reflect | Discuss what helped the group move forward | “What did we learn from the mistake?” |

These are **design patterns**, not live features already implemented in Akor. Each requires content rules and appropriate authorized runtime behavior.

## How not to design interaction

- Do not build a “collaboration” score from the number of chat messages, speed, popularity, or how often a child speaks.
- Do not give the strongest reader all the decisive information while making another learner permanently dependent.
- Do not force public disclosure of learning needs or accessibility settings.
- Do not reward exclusion, teasing, domination, or competition for essential learning tools.
- Do not confuse a fictional role with a real permission level.
- Do not require real purchases, external messaging, or physical contact for a fictional task.

## Social safety and fairness

Cooperation should be **structured, not coerced**. Provide sentence starters, turn-taking support, teacher moderation, an optional private help path, and alternative methods of contribution. Children should be allowed to opt out of a particular social interaction for a safe, pedagogically acceptable route under teacher guidance.

Akor should make waiting, peer absence, and disagreement **explicitly recoverable states**, with a teacher override or an approved solo fallback. It must not automatically invent a peer's contribution, while a simulated helper must be clearly described as such.

## A useful design review

Before approving a scenario, ask:

- Could one child finish everything without ever needing a partner? If yes, is this really collaborative?
- Does every role affect the shared outcome?
- Can a child explain why they need another person's contribution?
- What happens if a child is quiet, absent, disconnected, or uses a different communication mode?
- Does the teacher get evidence of understanding rather than merely activity counts?
